RESULTS: FUNCTIONAL ENRICHMENT AND TRANSCRIPTIONAL RESPONSES OF MAIZE
METABOLIC ENZYME GENES UNDER ABIOTIC STRESS

Research question How do drought, salinity, heat, and cold stress alter
the expression of metabolic enzyme genes in maize, and which biological
processes show shared, stress-specific, or contrasting transcriptional
responses across these conditions?

IMPORTANT EDITORIAL NOTE This document is a results-section draft based
on the tables and numerical outputs supplied for the current analysis.
It is written to distinguish statistical enrichment from expression
direction and physiological activity. Before submission, reconcile the
text with the final figure files, the complete analysis scripts, the
exact GO annotation release, the DESeq2 design and contrast definitions,
and the multiple-testing correction procedure. Do not describe GO
biological processes as experimentally measured metabolic pathway
activity. The terms reported below are GO biological-process (GO BP)
terms.

1.  OVERVIEW OF THE ENRICHMENT ANALYSIS

Functional enrichment was performed on differentially expressed
metabolic enzyme genes identified under cold, drought, heat, and
salinity stress relative to the control condition. Differentially
expressed genes were defined using an adjusted p-value threshold of ≤
0.05 and an absolute log2 fold-change threshold of ≥ 1. The
metabolic-enzyme DEG sets contained 598 genes for cold, 919 for drought,
1,599 for heat, and 840 for salinity.

The enrichment background was constructed by intersecting the
metabolic-enzyme gene set, the genes tested in the DESeq2 analysis, and
the genes represented in the GO biological-process annotation. The
resulting background contained 12,051 genes. The input audit showed that
all genes in each stress-specific metabolic-enzyme DEG set were retained
in this background: 598/598 cold genes, 919/919 drought genes,
1,599/1,599 heat genes, and 840/840 salinity genes. No input genes were
excluded during this background-intersection step.

Across the four stress conditions, 11 GO biological-process terms met
the reported adjusted p-value threshold of 0.05. These comprised one
term under cold stress, five under drought stress, one under heat
stress, and four under salinity stress. Two GO terms were significantly
enriched in more than one stress condition: ethylene biosynthetic
process, identified under cold, drought, and salinity stress; and
trehalose biosynthetic process, identified under drought and salinity
stress.

The number of significant terms differed among stress conditions. This
difference should be interpreted as a difference in the enrichment
results obtained under the current analysis and thresholds, rather than
as a direct ranking of stress severity or the total biological impact of
each stress. The number of significant terms is influenced by the DEG
set, annotation coverage, gene-set size, effect sizes, and statistical
power.

3.  COLD-STRESS RESPONSE

One GO biological process was significantly enriched among the
cold-responsive metabolic-enzyme genes: ethylene biosynthetic process
(GO:0009693). The term contained 20 differentially expressed genes among
598 input genes, corresponding to a GeneRatio of 20/598. In the
enrichment background, 112 of 12,051 genes were associated with this
term. The reported RichFactor was 0.1786, FoldEnrichment was 3.5986, and
zScore was 6.3132. The nominal p-value was 5.3480 × 10^-7, with an
adjusted p-value of 1.8344 × 10^-4 and q-value of 1.7339 × 10^-4.

Expression-direction analysis showed that 18 of the 20 genes (90.0%)
were upregulated, whereas two genes (10.0%) were downregulated. The
median log2 fold change was 3.3816 and the mean log2 fold change was
3.4664. Thus, the enriched ethylene-biosynthesis gene set displayed a
predominantly upregulated transcriptional pattern under cold stress.

These findings indicate that genes annotated to ethylene biosynthesis
were overrepresented among the cold-responsive metabolic-enzyme genes
and that most of the contributing genes showed increased transcript
abundance relative to the control. This result is consistent with a
transcriptional response involving ethylene-associated biosynthetic
functions under cold stress. It does not, by itself, demonstrate
increased ethylene concentration, ethylene production rate, or a causal
role for ethylene in cold tolerance.

4.  DROUGHT-STRESS RESPONSE

Five GO biological processes were significantly enriched among the
drought-responsive metabolic-enzyme genes. These included photosynthetic
electron transport in photosystem I, raffinose catabolic process,
ethylene biosynthetic process, trehalose biosynthetic process, and amine
catabolic process.

4.1 Photosynthetic electron transport in photosystem I

Photosynthetic electron transport in photosystem I (GO:0009773) was
represented by 10 genes among the 919 drought-responsive
metabolic-enzyme genes. The GeneRatio was 10/919, while 28 of the 12,051
background genes were annotated to this process. The RichFactor was
0.3571, FoldEnrichment was 4.6833, and zScore was 5.6062. The nominal
p-value was 2.3349 × 10^-5, and the adjusted p-value was 0.00876
(q-value = 0.00816).

All 10 genes contributing to this enriched term were downregulated. The
median log2 fold change was -1.7165 and the mean was -1.9385. The
uniform direction of the contributing genes provides a clear
transcriptional pattern: the photosystem I electron-transport-associated
genes in this enriched set showed reduced transcript abundance under
drought relative to the control.

This result should be described specifically as downregulation of genes
annotated to photosynthetic electron transport in photosystem I. It
should not be presented as direct evidence that photosynthetic electron
transport or photosynthetic efficiency was measured or reduced, because
no direct physiological measurement is represented in this analysis.

4.2 Raffinose catabolic process

Raffinose catabolic process (GO:0034484) contained five genes among the
919 drought-responsive genes. Eight background genes were annotated to
the term. The GeneRatio was 5/919, RichFactor was 0.6250, FoldEnrichment
was 8.1957, and zScore was 5.8495. The nominal p-value was 1.1758 ×
10^-4, with an adjusted p-value of 0.02205 and q-value of 0.02055.

Two of the five contributing genes were upregulated and three were
downregulated. The upregulated proportion was 0.40 and the downregulated
proportion was 0.60. The median log2 fold change was -2.7211 and the
mean was -2.2742. Under the analysis’ 70% directional classification
rule, this term was classified as mixed rather than predominantly
upregulated or downregulated.

The enrichment result indicates overrepresentation of
raffinose-catabolism-associated genes among the drought-responsive gene
set. However, the mixed directions mean that the term should not be
described as uniformly activated or repressed at the transcript level.
The negative mean and median log2 fold changes provide a summary
tendency, but do not override the mixed gene-level pattern.

4.3 Ethylene biosynthetic process

Ethylene biosynthetic process (GO:0009693) was also enriched under
drought stress. Twenty genes were represented among the 919
drought-responsive genes, compared with 112 genes annotated to this term
in the 12,051-gene background. The RichFactor was 0.1786, FoldEnrichment
was 2.3416, and zScore was 4.0985. The nominal p-value was 2.7955 ×
10^-4, with an adjusted p-value of 0.03417 and q-value of 0.03184.

Twelve of the 20 genes were upregulated (60.0%) and eight were
downregulated (40.0%). The median log2 fold change was 2.2443 and the
mean was 1.7586. Because neither direction reached the 70% threshold,
the term was classified as mixed.

Ethylene biosynthesis was therefore significantly enriched under
drought, but the contributing genes did not show the same degree of
directional consistency observed under cold and salinity stress. The
positive mean and median fold changes indicate a positive central
tendency, while the gene counts support the more cautious classification
of a mixed response.

4.4 Trehalose biosynthetic process

Trehalose biosynthetic process (GO:0005992) contained nine genes among
the drought-responsive genes, while 31 background genes were annotated
to this term. The GeneRatio was 9/919, RichFactor was 0.2903,
FoldEnrichment was 3.8070, and zScore was 4.4962. The nominal p-value
was 3.6446 × 10^-4, with an adjusted p-value of 0.03417 and q-value of
0.03184.

All nine contributing genes were downregulated. The median log2 fold
change was -3.4269 and the mean was -3.2875. This represents a uniformly
downregulated direction among the genes contributing to the enriched
term in this dataset.

The result identifies trehalose-biosynthesis-associated genes as an
enriched group among drought-responsive metabolic-enzyme genes, with a
consistent negative expression direction. It does not establish a
decrease in trehalose abundance or metabolic flux, which would require
metabolite or biochemical measurements.

4.5 Amine catabolic process

Amine catabolic process (GO:0009310) contained five genes among the
drought-responsive genes, compared with 10 annotated genes in the
background. The RichFactor was 0.5000, FoldEnrichment was 6.5566, and
zScore was 5.0506. The nominal p-value was 4.6474 × 10^-4, with an
adjusted p-value of 0.03486 and q-value of 0.03248.

One gene was upregulated and four were downregulated, corresponding to
20.0% and 80.0%, respectively. The median log2 fold change was -1.5461
and the mean was -0.8467. The term was therefore classified as
predominantly downregulated using the 70% threshold.

Overall, drought stress was associated with enrichment of several
biological processes with different expression patterns. The strongest
directional consistency was observed for photosystem I
electron-transport-associated genes and
trehalose-biosynthesis-associated genes, both of which were uniformly
downregulated in their respective enriched gene sets. Amine catabolism
was also predominantly downregulated. Raffinose catabolism and ethylene
biosynthesis showed mixed gene-level directions.

5.  HEAT-STRESS RESPONSE

One GO biological process, protein folding (GO:0006457), was
significantly enriched among the heat-responsive metabolic-enzyme genes.
The term contained 46 genes among the 1,599 heat-responsive genes, while
188 of the 12,051 background genes were annotated to protein folding.
The GeneRatio was 46/1,599, RichFactor was 0.2447, FoldEnrichment was
1.8441, and zScore was 4.5622. The nominal p-value was 2.0968 × 10^-5,
with an adjusted p-value of 0.01002 and q-value of 0.00945.

Forty-one of the 46 genes were upregulated (89.1%), while five were
downregulated (10.9%). The median log2 fold change was 3.1988 and the
mean was 3.4639. The term was therefore classified as predominantly
upregulated.

The result indicates that genes annotated to protein folding were
overrepresented among the heat-responsive metabolic-enzyme gene set,
with most contributing genes showing increased transcript abundance.
This pattern is compatible with a heat-associated transcriptional
response involving protein folding and proteostasis-related functions.
However, the GO term should be reported as a biological process rather
than a canonical metabolic pathway. The analysis does not directly
measure protein folding capacity, protein damage, chaperone activity, or
cellular proteostasis.

6.  SALINITY-STRESS RESPONSE

Four GO biological processes were significantly enriched among the
salinity-responsive metabolic-enzyme genes: lignin biosynthetic process,
jasmonic acid biosynthetic process, trehalose biosynthetic process, and
ethylene biosynthetic process.

6.1 Lignin biosynthetic process

Lignin biosynthetic process (GO:0009809) contained 16 genes among the
840 salinity-responsive genes. In the background, 63 genes were
annotated to this process. The GeneRatio was 16/840, RichFactor was
0.2540, FoldEnrichment was 3.6435, and zScore was 5.7583. The nominal
p-value was 4.4102 × 10^-6, with an adjusted p-value of 0.001596 and
q-value of 0.001504.

Fifteen of the 16 genes were upregulated (93.75%), while one was
downregulated (6.25%). The median log2 fold change was 2.9084 and the
mean was 2.7139. This represents a predominantly upregulated expression
pattern among the genes contributing to the enriched lignin-biosynthesis
term.

The result indicates overrepresentation and predominantly increased
expression of lignin-biosynthesis-associated genes under salinity
stress. It is consistent with a transcriptional response involving
cell-wall-related biosynthetic functions. It does not establish
increased lignin content or cell-wall deposition, which would require
direct biochemical or structural measurements.

6.2 Jasmonic acid biosynthetic process

Jasmonic acid biosynthetic process (GO:0009695) contained 25 genes among
the 840 salinity-responsive genes, compared with 140 annotated genes in
the background. The GeneRatio was 25/840, RichFactor was 0.1786,
FoldEnrichment was 2.5619, and zScore was 5.0880. The nominal p-value
was 1.1273 × 10^-5, with an adjusted p-value of 0.002041 and q-value of
0.001923.

Twenty genes were upregulated (80.0%) and five were downregulated
(20.0%). The median log2 fold change was 2.7149 and the mean was 2.2883.
The term was classified as predominantly upregulated.

This enrichment indicates that jasmonic-acid-biosynthesis-associated
genes were overrepresented among the salinity-responsive
metabolic-enzyme genes, with a majority showing increased transcript
abundance. The result is consistent with a transcriptional response
involving jasmonate-associated biosynthetic processes. It does not
demonstrate increased jasmonic acid concentration or prove that
jasmonate signalling caused the observed stress response.

6.3 Trehalose biosynthetic process

Trehalose biosynthetic process (GO:0005992) contained nine genes among
the 840 salinity-responsive genes. Thirty-one background genes were
annotated to the term. The GeneRatio was 9/840, RichFactor was 0.2903,
FoldEnrichment was 4.1651, and zScore was 4.8298. The nominal p-value
was 1.8537 × 10^-4, with an adjusted p-value of 0.02237 and q-value of
0.02107.

One gene was upregulated and eight were downregulated, corresponding to
11.1% and 88.9%, respectively. The median log2 fold change was -2.3138
and the mean was -1.8596. The term was therefore predominantly
downregulated.

This finding is notable because trehalose biosynthetic process was also
significantly enriched under drought stress, where all nine contributing
genes were downregulated. The repeated enrichment and predominantly
negative direction across drought and salinity suggest a shared
transcriptional pattern among trehalose-biosynthesis-associated genes
under these two conditions. This should not be interpreted as direct
evidence of reduced trehalose content or metabolic flux.

6.4 Ethylene biosynthetic process

Ethylene biosynthetic process (GO:0009693) contained 19 genes among the
840 salinity-responsive genes, compared with 112 annotated genes in the
background. The GeneRatio was 19/840, RichFactor was 0.1696,
FoldEnrichment was 2.4338, and zScore was 4.1727. The nominal p-value
was 2.4929 × 10^-4, with an adjusted p-value of 0.02256 and q-value of
0.02125.

Eighteen of the 19 genes were upregulated (94.7%), while one was
downregulated (5.3%). The median log2 fold change was 2.9599 and the
mean was 3.4608. The term was classified as predominantly upregulated.

Together with the cold-stress result, this supports a repeated
enrichment of ethylene-biosynthesis-associated genes with predominantly
positive expression direction in cold and salinity conditions. Under
drought, the same GO term was enriched but showed a mixed gene-level
direction. The shared GO annotation therefore does not imply an
identical response across all stresses.

7.  CROSS-STRESS COMPARISON OF ENRICHED BIOLOGICAL PROCESSES

The cross-stress comparison identified two GO biological processes that
were significantly enriched in more than one stress condition: ethylene
biosynthetic process and trehalose biosynthetic process.

7.1 Ethylene biosynthetic process: enrichment across three stresses

Ethylene biosynthetic process (GO:0009693) was significantly enriched
under cold, drought, and salinity stress. Under cold stress, 18 of 20
contributing genes were upregulated (90.0%). Under salinity stress, 18
of 19 were upregulated (94.7%). Under drought stress, 12 of 20 were
upregulated (60.0%) and eight were downregulated (40.0%), resulting in a
mixed classification under the 70% rule.

This pattern indicates that ethylene-biosynthesis-associated genes were
repeatedly overrepresented among the responsive metabolic-enzyme genes
under three stress conditions. The direction was predominantly positive
under cold and salinity, whereas drought showed a mixed response. Thus,
the evidence supports shared enrichment of the biological process, but
not a uniform direction of expression across all three stresses.

7.2 Trehalose biosynthetic process: shared downregulated pattern

Trehalose biosynthetic process (GO:0005992) was significantly enriched
under drought and salinity stress. All nine contributing genes were
downregulated under drought, while eight of nine were downregulated
under salinity. The median log2 fold changes were -3.4269 and -2.3138,
respectively.

The consistency of the predominant negative direction across these two
conditions identifies trehalose biosynthesis as a shared transcriptional
response in the drought and salinity comparisons. This is a descriptive
cross-stress observation based on the enriched gene sets. It does not
establish that the same nine genes were involved in both conditions; the
overlap of the underlying gene lists should be quantified separately
before making that claim.

7.3 Processes detected in only one stress condition

The remaining six GO terms were significant in only one of the four
stress comparisons:

-   Drought: photosynthetic electron transport in photosystem I,
    raffinose catabolic process, and amine catabolic process.
-   Heat: protein folding.
-   Salinity: lignin biosynthetic process and jasmonic acid biosynthetic
    process.

Cold had no additional significant GO term beyond ethylene biosynthetic
process.

The phrase “detected in only one stress condition” is used deliberately.
A term that reaches statistical significance in one stress but not
another is not necessarily significantly different between the two
stresses. Therefore, these results identify stress-associated enrichment
patterns in the present dataset, not statistically proven
stress-exclusive biological processes.

8.  CONTRASTING AND OPPOSING TRANSCRIPTIONAL RESPONSES

The cross-stress direction analysis did not identify any shared enriched
GO process with opposing predominant directions under the current
classification rule. Ethylene biosynthetic process was predominantly
upregulated under cold and salinity, while drought showed a mixed
pattern. Trehalose biosynthetic process was predominantly downregulated
under both drought and salinity.

Accordingly, the present results provide evidence of shared enrichment
and some differences in directional consistency, but they do not
demonstrate a statistically significant opposing response between stress
conditions. In particular, a mixed direction in one stress should not be
treated as proof of an opposite response to another stress. A formal
test of stress-by-process differences, or a gene-level comparison
accounting for uncertainty in the fold-change estimates, would be
required to establish statistically supported opposing responses.

The absence of opposing directions in this summary is limited to the GO
terms that were significantly enriched in more than one stress and to
the descriptive direction-classification procedure. It does not exclude
contrasting expression responses among other metabolic-enzyme genes or
among biological processes that did not pass the enrichment threshold.

9.  FIGURE AND TABLE PRESENTATION IN THE MANUSCRIPT

Figures and tables are complementary, not interchangeable. Tables
provide exact numerical values, gene counts, enrichment statistics, and
direction summaries. Figures communicate patterns visually. A figure
should not be described as containing all the same information as its
underlying table unless every value is explicitly displayed.

Suggested presentation:

Figure 1. Stress-specific GO biological-process enrichment of maize
metabolic-enzyme genes. A dot plot or bubble plot showing the
significantly enriched GO BP terms for each stress. The x-axis may show
GeneRatio or FoldEnrichment, point size may represent the number of
contributing genes, and point colour may represent adjusted p-value. The
figure should clearly identify the stress associated with each term. Use
the exact GO terms and adjusted p-values from the enrichment table.

Figure 2. Directional expression patterns of genes contributing to
significantly enriched GO BP terms. A heatmap or grouped bar plot
showing the proportion of upregulated and downregulated genes for each
enriched GO term and stress. If a heatmap is used, include a legend
defining the direction categories and indicate that the direction labels
are descriptive and based on the 70% threshold. A heatmap of gene-level
log2 fold changes would be more informative than a summary-only heatmap
if the underlying gene-level data are available.

Figure 3. Cross-stress distribution of shared and stress-associated GO
biological processes. A matrix or tile plot showing which GO terms were
significantly enriched under cold, drought, heat, and salinity.
Distinguish significant enrichment from lack of significant enrichment.
Do not label a blank cell as “no biological response”; it means only
that the term was not significant in that stress comparison under the
applied analysis.

Suggested figure captions:

10. INTEGRATED INTERPRETATION

Taken together, the GO enrichment results indicate that the four abiotic
stress conditions were associated with distinguishable patterns of
biological-process enrichment among maize metabolic-enzyme genes. Cold
stress was characterised by enrichment of ethylene biosynthesis with a
predominantly upregulated gene set. Drought stress showed a broader set
of enriched processes, including downregulated photosystem I
electron-transport-associated genes and
trehalose-biosynthesis-associated genes, alongside mixed responses in
raffinose catabolism and ethylene biosynthesis. Heat stress was
associated with enrichment of protein folding, with most contributing
genes upregulated. Salinity stress was associated with enrichment of
lignin and jasmonic acid biosynthesis, both predominantly upregulated,
as well as ethylene biosynthesis, which was also predominantly
upregulated, and trehalose biosynthesis, which was predominantly
downregulated.

The cross-stress comparison highlighted ethylene biosynthesis and
trehalose biosynthesis as recurring biological processes. Ethylene
biosynthesis was enriched in cold, drought, and salinity conditions, but
its expression direction was predominantly upregulated only under cold
and salinity; drought showed a mixed pattern. Trehalose biosynthesis was
enriched under drought and salinity and showed predominantly
downregulated expression in both conditions. These findings suggest that
some biological-process annotations recur across stress conditions,
while the direction and consistency of the associated gene expression
can differ.

The results therefore support a model in which maize metabolic-enzyme
genes exhibit both shared and stress-associated transcriptional
responses under the four abiotic stress conditions. However, the
analysis is based on transcriptomic data and GO annotation. It
identifies overrepresentation of annotated biological processes and the
expression directions of contributing genes; it does not directly
establish changes in enzyme activity, metabolite concentrations,
metabolic flux, or whole-plant stress tolerance.

11. LIMITATIONS AND INTERPRETIVE BOUNDARIES

Several limitations should be acknowledged.

First, each stress condition was represented by two biological
replicates. Although the DESeq2 analysis and enrichment results provide
a structured comparison of the available samples, the limited
replication reduces the precision and generalisability of the findings.
The results should be interpreted as patterns in this dataset and should
be validated in additional biological material.

Second, GO enrichment depends on the completeness and specificity of the
annotation. Genes without GO BP annotation cannot contribute to the
enrichment test, and broad or overlapping GO categories can affect the
interpretation. GO terms are not necessarily independent because genes
may be assigned to multiple related biological processes.

Third, the analysis used a DEG threshold of adjusted p-value ≤ 0.05 and
absolute log2 fold change ≥ 1. Threshold-based enrichment can omit genes
with smaller but coordinated expression changes. A ranked enrichment
analysis using the full set of tested genes could provide a
complementary assessment, but it is not part of the results presented
here.

Fourth, the direction classification used a 70% threshold. This is a
descriptive rule for summarising the proportion of upregulated and
downregulated genes within an enriched term. It is not an independent
statistical test of pathway direction. The direction labels should
therefore be interpreted alongside the actual gene counts and
fold-change summaries.

Fifth, significance in one stress and non-significance in another does
not itself establish a significant difference between stresses.
Likewise, shared enrichment does not prove that the same genes drive the
process in each condition. Direct gene-overlap analysis and formal
cross-stress statistical comparisons would be needed to support those
stronger claims.

Finally, transcript abundance does not necessarily correspond to enzyme
abundance or activity. No direct metabolite, enzyme-activity,
metabolic-flux, or physiological measurements are represented in these
GO tables. Statements about biochemical outcomes should therefore remain
hypotheses or interpretations for future validation, not conclusions
established by this analysis.

12. CONCLUSION OF QUESTION 1

The GO biological-process enrichment analysis identified 11
significantly enriched processes among maize metabolic-enzyme genes
responsive to cold, drought, heat, and salinity stress. Ethylene
biosynthetic process was enriched under cold, drought, and salinity
stress, while trehalose biosynthetic process was enriched under drought
and salinity stress. The latter showed a consistent predominantly
downregulated transcriptional pattern in both conditions. Other notable
responses included downregulation of photosystem I
electron-transport-associated genes under drought, upregulation of
protein-folding-associated genes under heat, and predominantly
upregulated lignin- and jasmonic-acid-biosynthesis-associated genes
under salinity.

These results demonstrate both recurring and stress-associated GO
biological-process enrichment patterns across the four conditions. They
answer the revised research question at the level of transcriptomic
enrichment and gene-expression direction, while leaving direct
biochemical activity and causal stress-response mechanisms for further
investigation.
