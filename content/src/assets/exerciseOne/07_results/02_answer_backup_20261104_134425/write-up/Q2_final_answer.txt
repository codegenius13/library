QUESTION 2: PRIORITY METABOLIC ENZYME CANDIDATES AND POTENTIAL REGULATORY GENES
==================================================================================

Research question:
Which metabolic enzyme genes emerge as key candidates within the pathway responses,
and which candidate regulatory genes may be associated with their coordinated or
stress-specific expression patterns?

Analysis basis
- This synthesis reuses the existing differential-expression, candidate-ranking,
  cross-stress and transcription-factor tables. The RNA-seq pipeline was not rerun.
- Statistical significance was defined as adjusted p-value (FDR) < 0.05.
- Candidate ranking considers statistical significance and the magnitude of the
  log2 fold change. A large fold change alone is not sufficient evidence of importance.
- Annotation availability is incomplete; a gene without a functional annotation
  is not automatically excluded from consideration.

- Candidates significant in multiple stresses are useful priorities for shared

How the regulatory candidates should be interpreted:
- The TFs listed above are candidate regulators because their expression changes
  significantly under the same stress conditions in which metabolic responses occur.
- These data do not by themselves demonstrate direct TF binding to an enzyme gene,
  a regulatory edge, or causation. The lists are stress-level candidates, not validated
  TF-to-enzyme target pairs.
- Stronger evidence would require reliable gene-ID reconciliation, gene-level
  co-expression across biological replicates or samples, motif/promoter evidence,
  relevant TF-target resources, or experimental validation.

4. Integrated conclusion
------------------------
The strongest enzyme candidates are the significantly differentially expressed genes
prioritized within each stress by adjusted p-value and effect size. Genes significant
across multiple stresses provide priorities for shared responses, while genes with
significance confined to one stress are useful candidates for stress-associated responses.
Directionally consistent multi-stress genes may participate in common response programs;
genes with opposing directions may reflect stress-dependent regulation or different
physiological contexts. These interpretations should be refined using functional and
pathway annotations rather than expression magnitude alone.

The significant TFs and enriched TF families provide a complementary set of candidate
regulatory genes for follow-up. Their stress-associated expression makes them plausible
participants in transcriptional responses, but the present results do not establish
which TF directly regulates which metabolic enzyme gene. Accordingly, the most defensible
outcome of Question 2 is a prioritized list of enzyme candidates plus a stress-specific
list of TF candidates for subsequent co-expression, promoter and regulatory-network analysis.

Limitations
- Functional annotations are incomplete and some candidate genes may remain uncharacterized.
- Gene-ID mapping differences or ambiguous mappings can affect the identity of TF candidates.
- Differential expression and shared stress response are associations, not proof of causation.
- A formal claim that a response is stress-specific should ideally be tested using
  contrasts or interaction models that directly compare stresses.
